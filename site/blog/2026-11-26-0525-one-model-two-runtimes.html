One model, two runtimes

2026-11-26 · nemotron · runtimes

Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe 30B-A4B now runs on this M5 Max two ways: MLX Audio on the NVFP4 checkpoint (the arm we measured first), and tonight NVIDIA's own NeMo-Speech.cpp 0.3.0 on the publisher's IQ4_NL GGUF. Both arms ran the same 19-job matrix — 12 corpus clips, 4 synthetic multi-speaker clips, 3 human recordings. All 19 jobs returned ok on both.

The numbers

On the corpus clips the two runtimes almost never disagree: word error between their transcripts lands at 0.6 to 6.3 percent, median about 3.3. Reading that as "the 4-bit GGUF costs nothing on real speech" is an observation, not a proof — but whatever the IQ4_NL conversion does to the weights, it does not change the words much.

Speed is not close. MLX Audio runs the corpus at rtf 0.026 to 0.068; speech.cpp needs 0.067 to 0.143 — two to three times slower. The human recordings stretch the gap further (0.15 to 0.41 against 0.03 to 0.05). If you want words fast, the MLX route keeps its crown.

Where speech.cpp wins

Diarisation. On the synthetic multi-speaker clips the MLX Audio arm collapsed two-voice material to one speaker. speech.cpp's encoder carries a speaker branch, and with --diarize it returns the right counts: 2→2, 3→2, 2→2 and 2→2. Per-word speaker labels come out of the same call. The old ensemble suggestion — this model for words, MOSS for speaker boundaries — now has a cheaper form: one runtime that gives you both.

DFlash2, NVIDIA's draft model for speculative decoding, buys nothing here: the Metal build runs upstream llama.cpp's standard path, which does no speculative decoding. Checked against the official docs/salm/models.md.

What failed on the way

The 19 GiB decoder download stalled once mid-run and needed a nudge; the first smoke test failed in 26 ms because --output-dir is directory-input-only — single files emit JSON on stdout; and the first scorer assumed text segments where the runtime emits one entry per word with a speaker key. All three cost minutes, not hours, and the fixes are in the runner and scorer (commits a80fe3d, 2044033).

Both GGUF files hash-match the publisher's blobs. The dashboard row for this arm is queued for the next presentation pass.
